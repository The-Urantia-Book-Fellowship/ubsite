# Decisions on the record

What was chosen, by whom, and why: Tigran's rulings above all, each with its date, and the reasons that a later change must not undo without asking. Until 2026-10-26 this was §8 of CLAUDE.md, in the order the decisions were made; it is grouped here by what they are about. `CLAUDE.md` §8 keeps the rules that bind every session. How each feature is built is in `ARCHITECTURE.md`. A § or a trap number names `CLAUDE.md`'s.

## The text on the page

- **The text is justified, as the book is, and breaks a word only where the book does** (Tigran, 2026-10-12, choosing the book's own hyphenation over pyphen's or the browser's). Settings' Alignment is Justified by default, or Ragged right (key `align`), because WCAG 1.4.8 asks for a way to unjustified text. The 144 verse lines stay start-aligned (`p.t.verse`), and 0:12.10 stays centred.
  - **A browser is not TeX.** It fills each line greedily and has no penalty for a hyphen, or for two running, so loose lines and more hyphens than print are the price, worst on a phone. Safari alone takes `-webkit-hyphenate-limit-lines: 1`.
  - **The cards and endnotes are set the same way** (Tigran, 2026-10-18), a 1955 reading's and a study note's: each note has `footnote.html_hy`, and each of the committee's reasons `srt_item.why_html_hy`, the same HTML with the book's soft hyphens, which only the card and the endnote use; the API and the lists keep `html`. `check_db` holds each to its `html` but for its soft hyphens, every one inside a word. Ragged right turns them off with the text.
- **A dot-leader row is split at its leader on the section page** (2026-10-12). `section.html` makes it a `.leadrow` flex line of the words (`.lab`) and the figure (`.fig`, which begins with the dots); the paragraph around it is an ordinary block. So its reference and ▶ stand as every other paragraph's do, and a row too long for its line breaks before the figure, dots and all, at any width. Before this, the paragraph itself was the flex line:
  - on a phone the reference and ▶ stood side by side and pushed 15:2's long rows past the screen, and the phone's `1fr` column grew with them, taking the whole section along. That column is now `minmax(0, 1fr)`.
  - once the text was justified, the words of a long row were spread across it.
  - `check_db` holds every leader outside all other spans, one to a paragraph, so that each half is whole HTML. The stored text and the API are untouched.
- **A paper's head is centred, as the book sets it** (Tigran, 2026-10-12): its number, title and author, and the section's title (the Foreword has no number, as in print: "Paper 0" stood over it from the first commit until Tigran noticed, 2026-10-20, and a reading path's running head and signpost say "Foreword" alone too); until 2026-10-18 the Listen button stood under them (below). It is as wide as the text column, never the margin as well, so it is centred over the text in both Widths. So is 0:12.10 ACKNOWLEDGMENT, the one heading within the text: the press reader marks it `paragraph.heading` from its `\hspace*{0.15\columnwidth}`, and a gate holds it to that one paragraph. An introduction has no title over its text; the left rail names it "0. Introduction", numbered as a reference like 13:0.2 numbers it.
- **A paper opens with its first letter dropped two lines, as the press sets it** (Tigran, 2026-10-13), the Foreword too: the press's `\lettrine{T}{}` (`ubook.sty`), with nothing after the letter set otherwise. It is CSS alone, `p.t.opening::first-letter` with `initial-letter: 2`, so a copy still takes the letter as text; Chrome, Edge and Safari draw it, and Firefox sets the letter plain. `test_opening.py` holds every opening to a paragraph that begins with a capital. It needed three things:
  - `font-size-adjust: none` on the letter. Under the device typefaces' adjust, Chrome 154 set it at the text's size, at the start of the second line.
  - A bottom margin of −0.3em, so that Goudy's J (ten papers) hangs its tail above the third line, as in print, instead of pushing that line in. Every letter still clears the second line, at every spacing.
  - The reference, ▶ and 1955 page in a line of their own above the paragraph, as a verse block's: on a phone from the first, and at every width since 2026-10-20 (Tigran: "except the first paragraph of the paper which has a real lettrine -- there we put them on top"). Floated in the corner, they sent the second line into the letter. The book prints no number at a paper's first paragraph either.
- **The book's text is black** (Tigran, 2026-10-13): `--text`, `#000` in light mode, for its paragraphs, a paper's and a section's titles, a part's page and a 1955 reading. `--ink`, a very dark brown, stays for everything around them; dark mode is unchanged.
- **Verse is set as the book sets it** (Tigran, 2026-10-12). The press sets the psalm in 48:6, the song in 122:9, the Lord's Prayer in 144:3 and the seven prayers of 144:5 in `quoting` blocks, one line to a paragraph; until 2026-10-12 the site set each line as a paragraph of prose. `readers/press.py` now keeps each block's first line (`paragraph.verse_start`, 10) and the lines the press sets in with `\hsetoff` (`setoff`, 4: 144:3.4, .6, .8, .10); an `\hsetoff` anywhere else fails the build. `queries.section` groups a section's paragraphs into `rows`, a paragraph alone or a block's lines.
  - The section page indents a block as one, with a blank line above and below, its lines close, a line's wrapped part hanging further in, and a set-in line further still, measured off the press's own page (ub.css `.verse`). Ragged, not justified: a browser justifies one short line at a time, badly.
  - **References**: only a block's first line shows its reference, play button and 1955 page, in a line of their own above the block, as the book numbers only a block's first line; the others are still there to land on. So on a phone from the first, and at every width since 2026-10-20 (below, "Each paragraph is marked"); until then a desktop kept every line's reference in the gutter, with its play button in the indent before the line (Tigran's choice, 2026-10-12).
  - **Index entries** citing a block's lines stand beside the block, each line's under its reference, so that a line cited six times (144:3.9) opens no gap in the verse. An interim: where the index's entries stand at all, for prose as well, is to be redesigned so that they never stand in the way of reading (Tigran, 2026-10-12), as a change of its own.
- **A separator is drawn where the press prints one** (Tigran, 2026-10-12, on finding that the site showed none). The press sets eleven: `\separatorline`, five stars set wider, before the closing notes of Parts I–III (31:10.22, 56:10.23, 119:8.9), and `\separatorshort`, three, in 120:3, 134:6 and six times between the prayers of 144:5. Until 2026-10-12 `readers/press.py` skipped them as layout. Each is now kept on the paragraph after it, `paragraph.sep_before`, and `build/foundation.py` holds them to the Foundation's ten: the same places and stars, but 119:8.9, which is the press's own (`tests/known-separator-deltas.tsv`).
  - The section page draws each as a row of its own across the grid, laid on the grid's columns (`subgrid`), so the stars stand centred over the text column, at every width and with the margin off. They are generated content (`.stars`), like the pilcrow, so a copy never takes them.
  - **The API sends each as `separator_before`** on the paragraph after it (version 5, below). Whether the edition takes the Foundation's numbering for the separators and for 139:9–10 is not decided (see trap 18).
- **A `\pc` paragraph is marked with a pilcrow** before its first word, faint like the paragraph numbers. It replaced an extra .85rem of space above the paragraph, which nobody could see (Tigran, 2026-10-06). It is the typeface's own ¶ (U+00B6), UB Goudy's with Goudy, as CSS generated content of an empty span: never a character in the text, so copying a paragraph still returns its text exactly. Until 2026-10-08 it was a drawn SVG (one of the logo candidates) used as a mask; Tigran asked why not the Unicode symbol, and the font's own is lighter, matches the face, follows the Typeface setting, and prints, which a mask did not. The API sends it as `group_before` (version 5).
- **Each paragraph is marked with its full reference**, `118:10.13`, not its bare number, so the reader always knows which paragraph this is (Tigran, 2026-10-09). It stands at the start of the paragraph with ▶ under it, and the 1955 page under that, like a drop capital, as wide as the section's longest reference (`--refw`, set in `section.html`): on a phone from the first, and at every width since 2026-10-20 (Tigran: "always the same as we do on the phone"). Until then a desktop kept them in a gutter, 4.4rem between the rail and the text, which stood empty with them turned off; the gap is 2.4rem now. The three are one float, `.stk` (`_para.html`; `listen.js` puts ▶ in it), so that a list's label can float beside it on the first line, and its parts float inside it, as they floated in the paragraph: as blocks, a copy put the reference on a line of its own. Each part is one line of the text tall (`--lh`, the line as a length), so the reference stands on the first line and ▶ is centred on the second, with the 1955 page beside it: the stack is never more than two lines tall, and the third line is the text's whole width (Tigran, the same day). The reference and the page are set right, the reference's right edge over the page's, as the gutter set the reference (Tigran, the same day); in the line above a paper's opening or a block of verse they stay left, from the text's edge. Until then ▶ followed the reference's own small line, close under it, with most of the second line empty below, and the page took a third line. Settings' "Paragraph references" (key `paranum`) turns it off.
- **A small indent where nothing stands at a paragraph's start** (Tigran, 2026-10-20): with the paragraph references, listening and the 1955 page references all off, each first line is set in 1em, as the book's are (its 8pt at 10pt, and a little more); the stack is an indent of its own when it stands there ("when the references or audio playback is turned on there is no need for extra indentation as they serve this purpose already"). A reading path, which has no play buttons, is indented with the references and pages off. Not indented: a paper's opening, the heading in the text, a line of verse, a list's item. The paragraphs stay spaced and the pilcrows stay: a first version closed the paragraphs up, as the press sets them, with a blank line where the pilcrow stands, and Tigran found that with everything off the pilcrows then made no visible difference, which confused.
- **A list's label stands in a box of its own, as `\ublistelem` sets it** (Tigran, 2026-10-20), in every layout: right-aligned in 2.2em, then .6em that neither stretches nor breaks, so that every item's words begin at one place. The press: `\kern1em\makebox[0.2em][r]{#1}\kern0.1em` and `\bibnobreakspace` (5pt), after the 8pt indent.
  - **The box floats**, one line tall, beside the stack. The space after the label is the text's own and stays in it, but the browser draws none at a line's start beside a float.
  - **A float drops that space from a copy too** ("2.By the will"). So `ub.js` sets `.copying` on `<html>` while the browser copies, and the CSS lifts the float for that moment: plain text and HTML byte-identical to the page without it (checked 2026-10-20).
  - A pilcrow before a label stands at the box's start. Every one of the 2,397 labels begins its paragraph; the widest, "“10.", is 1.76em in Goudy.
  - An absolutely placed label was tried, and Chrome placed it at the paragraph's edge, ignoring the floats and the indent. A label floated after the stack's parts, each a float of its own, could stand no higher than the last of them: hence `.stk`.
- **UB Goudy is OFL 1.1** — verified in the name table, a correctly renamed Sorts Mill Goudy derivative — so the site serves the book's own face, subsetted to the corpus with a gate that fails on a missing glyph. **Goudy Handtooled BT is Bitstream proprietary and must never be served.**
- **Goudy is set large** (2026-09-21). Everything through Phase 5 is committed in seven commits, the last four of which are typography: the site was set too small and wanted 150% browser zoom to be comfortable, which is a Goudy problem — small x-height, long descenders, so it reads a full step below its nominal size.
- **A link is grey around the reading and blue where going somewhere is the page's job** (Tigran, 2026-10-12). Grey: the header, the rail, the margin index, the box of entries cited across a section, all on screen the whole time. Blue: the Contents page, the index's letters, the Chronology's periods, the transport bar, and the row at the top of each appendix (`_appnav.html`). That row was grey like the header until 2026-10-12, so on a phone, with no hover, its letters read as a caption; each letter is now padded to a thumb's width. The concept page's "Listed in Appendix C, …" stays grey: it is a statement about the headword, and the link is extra.

## Moving about the book

- **`/101` redirects to `/101:0`.** Every paper opens with an introduction, and the section list is always in the left rail.
- **A paragraph address names paragraphs that exist, and its range runs forwards** (2026-10-13), as the API's references do. `/0:0.0-1`, `/86:5.18` and `/86:5.15-14` are not-found pages saying what is wrong ("0:0 runs from 0:0.1 to 0:0.6"); until then they opened the section with nothing marked. The search box sends a typed range whole, `86:5.14-15`, so the address explains a wrong one. The index's one backwards range, `39:3.9-8`, links to `/39:3.9`, and `check_db` holds every index link to a place that opens.
- **The left rail is the whole book, closed but for the paper being read** (Tigran, 2026-10-10). It lists the Foreword, the four parts, every paper and every section, as native `<details>` (`templates/_rail.html`). The part and the paper being read are open, so a reader can jump anywhere without scrolling back up. It is sticky beside the text on a wide screen and the first block of the page on a narrow one, scrolling away with the text (Tigran: "obviously"). **A page carries the tree down to the papers, plus its own paper's sections.** All 1,626 sections inline would add 111 KB raw (22 KB gzipped) to a section page of about 20 KB, and `listen.js` fetches whole pages when it swaps them. As built, the rail adds 22 KB raw, 3.5 KB gzipped. Another paper's sections come from `/rail/N` when it is opened (`ub.js`), asked for under `db_version()`, so the browser keeps them for good and never shows a stale one; `/rail/` is not counted as a visit. With JavaScript off such a paper is a plain link, its chevron hidden (`.lazy`, and the `js` class the head script sets). `ub.js` scrolls the rail, never the page, to put the paper being read a sixth of the way down, again whenever `listen.js` swaps `<main>` (`ub:swap`). Never give the rail's list a class of `tree`: that is the concept page's, and its `li` rule indented every wrapped section title. **A part's title opens the part's own page**, `/part/N` (Tigran, 2026-10-10): its divider as the book prints it, `PART IV`, the title on its printed lines and the sponsors' note, and none of the text. The words come from the press style's `\part<N>txt`, `up`, `mix` and `sub`, read by `readers/press.py` `read_parts` into the `part` table; the chevron beside the title still only opens the part.
- **The left rail folds** (Tigran, 2026-10-13): away to the left on a wide screen, up to one line on a phone, by a button at its head, "Contents" with a chevron drawn as the summaries' are (`.railfold`, the first child of `nav.rail`).
  - **On a wide screen** it is open by default. Folded, the rail's column is gone and only its chevron stays, sticky like the rail, at the outer edge of the gap before the text, level with the header's mark (Tigran, the same day: a strip of its own left too much margin). So the text stands 3.6rem from the window's edge, the page padding and the 2.4rem gap; it stood 5.6rem until 2026-10-20, when the references left the 4.4rem gutter the gap was (above, "Each paragraph is marked"). In Full width the text column widens; in Measured the frame gives up what the rail did, so the page stays centred. The browser remembers it as the setting `rail` (`open`, `folded`), declared in `settings.py` `KEPT`: kept in `ub_settings` and applied before first paint like every setting, but not in the dialog, since the button is its control. "Restore the defaults" opens it.
  - **On a phone every page starts folded**, by CSS alone (`:root.js`), so the text is at the top; opening it is a class on the rail (`shown`) for that page only. Remembering it open would spread ~80 lines above the text of the page a reader tapped through to. A page `listen.js` swaps in comes folded too, its rail being new.
  - With JavaScript off there is no button and the rail is open, as before. The grid is not animated, since every frame would reflow a whole justified section; only the chevron turns.
- **The contents in a translation** (Tigran, 2026-10-27): a list on the rail's "Contents" line, at its end, offers "Uversa Press", as Compare names the English, and each translation ticked by its short name, in Settings' order; shown only while one is ticked. Chosen, the whole rail is that translation's own contents: its parts', papers' and sections' titles as its printed contents give them (ud's papers from its `paper-titles.tex`, in normal case where its text has capitals), and the rail's own words in its language. Kept as `toc` (settings.py KEPT), never sent to the server.
  - **The contents' own words are in the contents' language, never the interface's**: "0. Introduction", "Part I.", "9 and 10." in the book's, «0. Введение», «Часть I.», «9 и 10.» in a Russian translation's. Until then they were the interface's, and a reader of it in Russian read «0. Введение» among the book's English titles. A paper's section 0 is named by us: no source names it (Tigran).
  - **What a translation has not keeps its English, greyed**, so that the rail stays whole and every link works (ud has 45 papers of 197). What it writes in capitals, ud's section titles, is set in small capitals.
- **A section ends with a transport bar, icons alone** (Tigran, 2026-10-14): ⏪ a paper back, ⏮ a section back, ⏭ a section on, ⏩ a paper on, centred under the text, each named by its tooltip ("Next section: 101:6", "Previous paper: the Foreword"). It replaced "‹ previous" and "next ›", which stayed within the paper. A section steps across a paper's edge, 101:0 back to 100's last section, and a paper goes to its first section; part pages are not stops, as listening's "Next" goes; a part's own page has the ⏭ alone, at the right, to its first paper's first section, where it had "next ›" (Tigran, the same day). At the book's ends an icon stands greyed in its place, so the four never move. ⏮ ⏭ step one unit here as in the player, where the unit is a paragraph. At the end only, not under the paper's head: the rail is there. `app.py` `transport()` chooses the four, `templates/_transport.html` draws a button for both pages; `tests/test_transport.py` holds every section's to its neighbours.
- **The page turns by keys, and by a tap if a reader asks** (Tigran, 2026-10-20). He asked for the volume keys, as reading apps use them, and no web page can have them: Chrome on Android keeps them for the system (`ContentUiEventHandler.shouldPropagateKeyEvent`, read in Chromium's source), and iOS Safari has no such event.
  - **Keys**: where the book's text is (a section page, a reading path), Space, Page Down and → go on a screen, and Shift+Space, Page Up and ← back, from a keyboard, a page turner, or volume keys an Android app sends as Page Down (`ub.js turn()`).
  - **A screen** is what the window shows, less a line: the last whole line comes to the top, and going back the first whole line to the bottom, about 93% of the window.
  - **A tap**: Settings' "Tap to turn the page" (key `tappage`, off by default) does the same for a finger's tap on the text, the lower half of the screen on, the upper half back. Never a mouse's click, which selects words, nor a tap on a link, a button or the index, nor the tap that closes a card.
- **The edition's lists live under `/lists`** (Tigran, 2026-10-12), as a book's front matter names them: "List of 1955 readings" at `/lists/1955` now, the illustrations and the study notes with Phase 6. `/lists` names them all, and the home page links to them in one line. The List of study notes, `/lists/notes`, joined it on 2026-10-18 (Tigran): every study note on the site, by paper, under the words it stands after (`app.py list_notes`). The **Bibliography**, `/lists/bibliography`, on 2026-10-19 (Tigran): every work the study notes cite, once, by its key, the author's surname, with the notes citing it (`queries.bibliography()`), so that as the papers are reviewed the human sources the book draws on can be seen in one list. A key names one work in every paper, which the build holds (`build/study.py` `one_work_one_key`). A work that can be read freely in Tigran's library links its page there, on a line under it (Tigran, 2026-10-25; `ARCHITECTURE.md`, Study notes). **A list is named only while what it lists is shown** (Tigran, the same day): the home page's line and `/lists` mark each with its switch (ub.css `.n1955`, `.nst`), since with the 1955 readings off the list sent a reader to rings the text no longer drew. The list pages themselves answer an address asked for, as the index's do.
- **Appendices A–F live at `/appendix/A` … `/appendix/F`**, the letters of the printed volume (Tigran, 2026-10-09); `/index/A` was already the A–Z's letter A. An anchor there hashes what a row *is*, a name or an event's date and words, not what it cites, so an added citation moves no link. The three `conjunction of Jupiter and Saturn in Pisces` of 7 B.C. are one text on three dates, and three anchors.
- **Slugs are data, not a function.** `data/slugs.tsv` is checked in and append-only. A headword keeps its slug for ever; collisions get a recorded numeric suffix (11 so far). A permalink that moves because someone improved the slugifier is a broken permalink.

## The index beside the text

- **Index chips carry concepts, not numbers.** `path_plain` strips the citations and the terminal period; on paper the numbers *are* the entry, on screen the chip is a link.
- **An ordinal's suffix is a superscript in the index, as in the text** (Tigran, 2026-10-24). The index sets it `1\ts{st}` since its 0aeae38: 427 of them, in the A–Z and Appendix A.
  - **Every plain column reads `1st`, as before**, so slugs, anchors and search are unchanged. Each one the site shows as words has an `_html` sibling, `December 25<sup>th</sup>`: `headword_html`, `path_html`, `xref.target_html` and `chronology_event.label_html`, made by `index_macros.render(…, sup=True)`, with the superscript and nothing else, so a chip gains no italics. `check_db` holds each to strip back to its plain column. `text_html` and `line_html` have it too.
  - **A See is found and linked by its HTML** (`derive.links`), since `text_html` holds `mansion worlds: 1<sup>st</sup>`. A q.v. needs nothing: `xref.readings` drops a `1st.` list marker, and the name's end is matched.
  - **Every search hit shows its superscripts, the text's too** (Tigran's choice). An FTS5 snippet is its text verbatim between its cuts, so `app.py _snip` finds it in the text and sets each `<sup>` where `sups()` says it stands. FTS5 marks whole tokens and a superscript stands inside one, so a `<sup>` nests in a `<mark>`. The committee's reasons and today's words beside a 1955 reading (`_around`) are set the same way. Offline, `offline-core.js snip` does it from the places `offline/searchdb.py` stores, held to the server by the offline gate.
- **Blanket citations appear once** at the section head, never repeated under every paragraph. This is `read_pack.py`'s rule and the reason Paper 15's 470 citations are readable.
  - **An entry citing a whole paper appears once, above §0** (Tigran, 2026-10-17), and in no other section's box, as a range stands beside its first paragraph only. Until then *God* and *Universal Father* stood above 1:0, 1:1, 1:2 …: 4,457 of the boxes' 7,344 chips were such repeats, and 272 sections had a box for nothing else.
    - §0's box is one box with two headings, "Cited across this whole paper" and then "Cited across this whole section" (23 introductions have both). On a phone it folds to "… across this whole paper", "… across this whole section" or "… across the whole paper and this section".
    - `queries.section` returns them as `paperwide` (empty after §0) and `blanket`. An entry citing both the paper and §0 is listed under the paper only; none does today.
  - **The box is as wide as the text** (2026-10-17). Its measure was taken at its own .9rem, so it stood two thirds as wide as the text on a tablet, and in Measured on a wide screen. Only its list is set smaller now.
    - On a wide screen it takes the paper head's width, the text column's: `min(measure, max(100% − 26ch − 2rem, (100% − 2rem) / 2))`.
    - The `max()` is new too. Between 992 and about 1,150 px the grid's two tracks share what is left after the gap until the margin's reaches 26ch, and the head was narrower than the text there.
    - In Full width the box still spans the page.
- **A range stands once, beside its first paragraph** (Tigran, 2026-10-13). `language › limitations of human language (44:0.20-21)` stands beside 44:0.20, not 44:0.21 as well, and `0:0.1-4` once, not four times: 8,822 paragraph targets only continue a range, about one chip in five. The chip still opens the concept page, whose citation `/44:0.20-21` tints the whole range. `load.py` records the fact as `citation_target.opens`, from the parser's spans, since a flattened target cannot tell `20-21` from `20,21`; a comma list (`61:5.3,7`) is not a range and stands beside each (two paragraphs in a row were one, `61:4.3,4`, until the index wrote every such pair as a range, 2026-10-21); both ends of an inverted span (`39:3.9-8`) stand. `queries.CHIP_OPENS` selects on it. `para_citers` and `para_density` are unchanged, since what the index cites is unchanged. On the same day the margin stopped saying "no entry cites this paragraph precisely": a paragraph nothing cites has an empty margin, `<div class="a"></div>`, so that a phone draws no box for it (`.a:not(:empty)`).
- **A *See* and a *See also* are links where they stand** (Tigran, 2026-10-18), as a *q.v.* is, so that a reader need not scroll down to the Cross-references to follow one. The Cross-references list stays, every target gathered in one place. Both give a target the same link, `xref.href`, made once by `derive.links`: to the subentry's line when the target names one that resolves (266, highlighted by `.tree li:target`), else the headword's page. A clause can name 154 targets, so each is taken where it must stand, after its mark or the `"; "` after the one before, never found by a search; one that is not there fails the build (`see-unlinked`). `check_db` holds each to its place and every headword link to a page and line that exist.
- **An appendix chip never repeats the A–Z** (Tigran, 2026-10-09). A B–F entry stands beside a paragraph only when no entry of the same headword is already there: 131 of 1,865 remain (`CHIP_NEW` in `ubsite/queries.py`). What the other 1,734 carried, the kind of thing a headword names, is on its concept page instead, as "Listed in Appendix C, Celestial Beings, Individuals Named". Every Chronology chip stays, and carries its month: "January 14, 26 A.D.: Jesus baptized by John".
- **The Chronology is the printed list first** (Tigran, 2026-10-09). The log-scale timeline (ROADMAP item 4) is to be built on that page, never in place of it. In print a heading stands above its events and finishes seven of them ("Paul dies prior to", under 82 A.D.). An event's one line, `chronology_event.label`, therefore puts the date after those seven and before the rest, with "of" for a month without a day: "December of 25 A.D.".

## Search

- **An exact headword match wins the search.** bm25 has no notion that a query names an entry, so headwords are resolved separately: exact, then prefix, then full text.
- **Ctrl+X searches** (Tigran, 2026-10-23). With words selected it puts them in the header's search box, and with none it only goes there; either way the page goes up to the box, which takes the focus, ready for Enter. Escape in the box, while it still has that focus, goes back: what the box held, the place, the selection (Tigran's choice). The search box's tooltip names it, and `aria-keyshortcuts` too.
  - **The words** are what the page draws of the selection (`ub.js chosen()`), without two things. The soft hyphens go, which `unicode61` reads as a break in the word, so that "supernatural", with one in it, would be searched as "super natural". So does a paragraph's `.stk`: a triple click takes its reference along, which would match nothing; only a selection of the reference alone keeps it, and opens the paragraph.
  - **Cut is left alone** inside a field. On the page there is nothing to cut, so Ctrl+X costs nothing there. A layout that writes no Latin letters (Armenian, Russian) is caught by `ev.code`, as the browser's Cut is.

## The text API and the licence

- **The API's `ref` is ours; the Foundation's is beside it as `uf_ref`** (Tigran, 2026-10-11). The site, the index and every link use ours. Every translation, and so every site joining its translation to our English, uses the Foundation's, so `/api` tells them to join on `uf_ref`. 0:12.10 is 0:12.10 in both: the Foundation prints no number beside it, and its 1955 page, 16.7, is the place its neighbours leave (text version 4, 2026-10-12).
- **The API calls the English text public domain; credit is requested, not required** (Tigran, 2026-10-11). The credit line names the site's own base URL, taken from the request, so it follows the move to urantiabook.org.
- **The text is public domain; the edition as a whole, with its index, is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** (Tigran, 2026-10-14), the licence the press edition carries. It answers Harry's condition for Work offline, that the site's terms not put the book, the index with it, in the public domain (CLAUDE.md §10). Every page's footer says so (`base.html`), with `rel="license"`, so the offline copy carries it too, and so does `/api`. The API's `licence` object still speaks of the text alone, which is all it sends. Raised before it was deployed: a CC licence cannot be withdrawn once granted, the press repo's own CC BY-NC-SA covers its typesetting and tools, and the press's copyright page says the index "is used by permission".
- **A passage or a paper is asked for by reference, at two addresses** (Tigran, 2026-10-13). `/api/v1/paragraphs/` takes paragraphs, ranges and sections (`101:4.2-7,101:4.7-5.2,61:4`), and `/api/v1/papers/` takes whole papers (`0,101-103`).
  - **A bare number under `/paragraphs/` is a 400** that names `/papers/N`. The index writes `61:5.3,7` for two paragraphs of one section, and read as a list that would quietly be 61:5.3 and all of Paper 7.
  - **`?numbering=uf` reads the references as the Foundation's.** Translations quote by them, and without the switch its 144:5.50 would quietly be another prayer line. Its numbered separators name no paragraph, and are a 404.
  - **The reply carries the section, paper and part each paragraph stands in**, so a quotation can be headed without a second request.
  - **It has no `since`, `full` or `removed`**, so a sync client handed one fails rather than keeping its version.
- **A paragraph is sent as HTML alone, with no plain copy beside it** (Tigran, 2026-10-11). Stripping its tags gives the text exactly, which `check_db` holds, and the text has no `&`, `<` or `>` to unescape. A second copy saved consumers one regex, cost 0.33 MB gzipped, and could in principle disagree with the first. Dropping it made version 2.
- **A text version moves only when something a site receives changes**, and a number, once it may have reached a site, is never made again for other text. So the ledger is append-only, `tests/test_versions.py` holds it to HEAD's as a prefix, and `deploy.sh` refuses to ship while `data/text/` is uncommitted.
- **A paragraph record says how the book sets it** (Tigran, 2026-10-12): `separator_before`, the stars of a separator (`"* * *"`, `"* * * * *"`, else `null`); `group_before`, whether it begins a 1955 group, the site's ¶ (`true` or `false`); `kind`, `"prose"`, `"verse"` (the 144 lines of 48:6, 122:9, 144:3 and 144:5, set one to a line) or `"heading"` (0:12.10); and `setoff`, a line of verse set in further (the Lord's Prayer's four second lines). All four are on every record, so that every record has one shape, and so is `readings_1955` (version 8, below the 1955 readings' decision). The first two made text version 5; `kind`, missed then, made version 6 the same evening, and `setoff` version 7, with the verse set as the book sets it. A block of verse needs no field: it begins with a verse line after prose or after a separator, which `check_db` holds. Each resent all 14,586 paragraphs, so that the API, a day old, would carry everything the section page uses to set a paragraph before more sites held a copy. Only the soft hyphens (`html_hy`) stay out, by decision. `check_db`'s record round-trip holds all four to the database.

## Work offline

- **Work offline: the reader taps to update, and the browser's voice reads offline** (Tigran, 2026-10-13).   - **Update now reloads the page, and brings the worker** (Tigran, 2026-10-18), so that nothing needs a reload by hand, even offline straight after. It first asks the browser for `/sw.js` (`reg.update()`) and waits for a new worker to take over, which then fetches the copy (`offline.js` `newest()`); once the copy is in, it reloads the page, playing or not; with What's new open, when it closes. A failed update does not reload. The browser looks for a new worker only after a page has loaded: without `newest()`, `make e2e` found the old worker still in place after the reload, so an app kept open across a deploy and taken offline at once would have run the new copy with the old worker's routing and search.
  - **Unless a reader turns it off, the copy keeps itself up to date** (Tigran, 2026-10-25): Update now suits a reader who keeps the copy as it was until they choose, and most would forget it and find the copy "broken". Settings' "Keep the offline copy up to date" (key `autoupdate`, on by default, in Settings' tab Offline, which `ub.js` shows only with the cloud) has `offline.js` `keepUp()` update it as if Update now were tapped whenever a check finds it out of date with the site reached and the page in view: as a page loads (the hourly check), as the browser comes online, and as the page comes into view again (an app brought to the front). Only from a page the reader has open, never by background or periodic sync; `sw.js` is unchanged. At its end What's new opens over the menu, saying that the copy has just been updated and what changed since the copy before, and the page reloads once it is closed (`told()`): Tigran asked for What's new there, and it is the warning that the page will reload. With nothing for readers in the news, nothing is said and nothing reloaded; while listening, closing it reloads nothing. Not with the browser's data saver on, and an update that stopped, tapped or not, is not tried by itself again for an hour (`localStorage` `ub_offline_auto`), so that a broken copy on the server does not start a download on every page.
- **Work offline holds everything but the recordings** (Tigran, 2026-10-14). The switch was "Read offline"; it is "Work offline", and says that search works too. Every headword's reading path is in the copy, in both orders, since Tigran asked what was wrong with the 120 MB they cost: unpacked they are 132 MB, gzipped 27 MB to download, and the browser now keeps the copy gzipped, so they add 27 MB, not 132. The copy is 44 MB, to download and to keep. Tigran expects it to reach about 500 MB once the illustrations are in; Chrome lets an origin keep up to 60% of the disk, and Safari 17 the same, in a Home Screen app as in Safari, and `offline.js` asks for persistent storage.
  - **The page about the text API is in the copy; the API is not** (Tigran, 2026-10-24: it is only documentation, to be read on a train too). It names the address it is read at (`app.py api_docs`, `request.base_url`), in 13 places and its example replies, and the copy is one for every host, so it was left out from the first.
