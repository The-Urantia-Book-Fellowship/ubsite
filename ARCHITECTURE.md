# How ubsite is built

What is built, feature by feature: the modules and tables of each part, and how it works. Until 2026-10-26 this was §3 of CLAUDE.md, which keeps the layout of the tree and a map of this file. Why each thing is as it is, and who chose it, is in `DECISIONS.md`. A § or a trap number names `CLAUDE.md`'s.

## Listening

Since 2026-10-07. Every section can be heard in Merritt Horn's reading, and from any paragraph once his reading is aligned; and since 2026-11-12 the translation USGNY in Григорий Войнер's, in Russian:

- `build/audio.py` reads `data/audio/` into `audio_source`, `audio_file`, `audio_segment` and `audio_unread`. Each voice is a list of segments, `(file, start_ms, end_ms)`, one per paragraph plus a spoken heading. The player never knows whether a voice is one file per section or one per paragraph. Every human reading is loaded alike, by its key (`_load_human`; `mh` alone until 2026-11-12): `sources.toml`'s `[audio.<key>]` says where it is, how its files are found and named (`glob`, and `name`, a pattern whose groups are the paper, the section and a combined section's second number), and which translation it reads (`audio_source.translation`; none, the English); `<key>-files.tsv` lists the directory, `<key>-overrides.tsv` the files the rule does not claim, `<key>.tsv` the cuts, `<key>-unread.tsv` what is not read. A file neither the rule nor an override claims fails the build, and so do two files for one place.
- `build/speech.py` builds the `speech` table: the text as a voice is to read it, with numbers in words, list marks spoken and brackets dropped. It is gated so that no digit or symbol survives.
- `tools/align/` turns a recording into `data/audio/<key>.tsv`: Whisper, difflib, and cuts placed in pauses. `--voice` (`make align VOICE=voyner`, and so each target; Merritt Horn's with none) says which reading, and `voice.py` what follows from it: the language Whisper hears it in, its translation's, and the text it is matched to, the English's `speech` or the translation's `tr_speech`. In Russian a number is one token, `#`, in digits or in words and in any case (`ru.py`: the translations' spoken text keeps digits, the reader declines them, and Whisper writes either), which is what lets a list's mark be its paragraph's first word heard (CLAUDE.md trap 16) and "Глава первая" meet the heading's "1.". A reading that read its translation as it stood before today names the commits of its repository that its text lies between (`sources.toml` `edition`, the nearest first: Григорий Войнер's two, the refresh of April 2017 and the commit of 2014), and each paragraph is matched to its words at whichever is nearer what was heard in it (`edition.py read`), weighed with the paragraph before it as already chosen, since two commits may divide a sentence otherwise between two lines of verse (122:9.14-15); each commit is read through `_io` and placed by the translation's own addresses, section by section (`paragraphs`), and `report` counts how far today's text has moved from the text read. What was heard in a paragraph is known once it is cut (`voice.py heard`), so `align.py` cuts twice, first with every paragraph as the first commit has it; the cuts hardly depend on which.
- `tools/tts/` makes the AI voice: the listening test that chose it, and `make tts` (below, Where the recorded AI reading stands).
- `/audio/<key>/<name>` serves only names listed in `audio_file`, with Range support. A reader may keep a recording offline since 2026-11-04 (Work offline, below), when the worker answers it from the device.
- **Your browser's voice** (2026-10-08) needs no recording: the browser speaks the `speech` text itself (the Web Speech API), fetched from `/speech/<paper>:<section>` once that voice is chosen. It is a stopgap beside Merritt Horn until the recorded AI reading exists, then a third choice (`DECISIONS.md`, and below).
- **A translation's own recording** (2026-11-12; `DECISIONS.md`, Listening): Григорий Войнер's of USGNY (`urantia-ru`), 1,624 files, 154 hours, cut at the paragraph: 14,575 paragraphs and 1,624 headings. It is a voice of every section but 19:0 and 50:0, which no file reads (`voyner-unread.tsv`; `listen.js carryOn` goes on there in the voice `trKey` gives, the browser's, and back), like Merritt Horn's, its segments at our paragraphs, the translations being stored under our numbering; what differs is `tr`, the translation it reads, which `app.py section_audio()` sends with it and its language (`whole_audio` passes them on), and `translations_json()` lists under the translation (`voices`). By that `listen.js` keeps it out of the English text's voices (`choose`, and the English text's own in the bar's list), plays it from the translation's ▶ (`trButton`, `trKey`: the recording unless Settings names a browser's voice for the translation, `ub.js trChoice`; `trvoices` holds a browser voice's name, or `browser`), which stands in the translation's column (`trPlay`, on `ub:cmp`) and, since 2026-11-13, in the fold under a paragraph (`foldPlay`, on `ub:trfold`, which `ub.js trDraw` sends each time it draws a fold, the line that names one translation marked `data-play`: the tabs' row, or the translation's name), lists it first under the translation in the bar, whose list has each text's recordings and the browser's voices in its language as one list under it, the English first (`voices()`), names it "USGNY · Григорий Войнер" (`vname`), and marks the translation's line as the browser's reading of it does (`V.tr`, `ubTrRead`); `trOf()` knows a recording's key as it knows `browser:<key>`, so carrying on, a change of voice and a translation unticked go as they went. Settings' Voices is a menu against each text, the English first (2026-11-13; `settings.py` `voice`, kind `voices`, whose `groups()` gives each text the recordings of it, the English those with no translation): `base.html` draws each menu with its recordings (`option[data-rec]`) and one entry that stands for the browser's voices until it lists them (`option.any`), and `ub.js listVoices` puts the browser's voices in the text's language after them by name, shows a translation's row while it is ticked, in the reader's order, and selects what reads the text now; a pick is kept by the menus' own listener, the English text's as `voice` and, a browser's, `bvoice`, a translation's in `trvoices`, the three settings each in a hidden input of the block, since every setting has a control of its name in its panel (`tests/test_settings_dialog.py`). A browser that cannot speak still has the recording's ▶ and its menu. The gate holds each voice to its own numbers (`verify/expectations.toml [audio.<key>]`), its pace in the words of the text it reads, and its `<key>-low-match.tsv` (`check_audio.py`). `tools/listen_tr_e2e.py` drives it in Chrome, Whole paper off and on; `tests/test_readings.py` holds the static facts.
- **A file sent with its length** (2026-11-12; CLAUDE.md trap 48): `build/mp3.py` walks an MP3's frames (`scan`), says whether a player would misjudge its length (`misjudged`: no Xing/Info frame, and its bytes at the bit rate named not its length by more than a frame) and makes the Info frame that puts it right (`info_frame`: a silent frame of the file's own kind holding the frame count and the byte count, and no table of contents, whose entries are whole 256ths of the file). `tools/audio/manifest.py` lists such files in `data/audio/<key>-frames.tsv`, where the ID3v2 tag ends, the frames, their bytes, the first header and the SHA-256 of the file as sent, and gives each its frames' duration in `<key>-files.tsv`; `build/audio.py info_frames` makes the frame, holds it to the listed duration, and stores it (`audio_file.head_at`, `head`, `sent_sha256`, all NULL for a file sent as it is). `app.py audio_file` sends such a file through `_with_head`, the file up to the tag's end, the frame, the rest, whole or by one range (206, 416, If-Range), from a response of its own, `_Pieces`; `_audio_list` gives the offline list each file's size and digest as sent, which is what the worker holds a kept file to. The gate asks that each stored frame be one; `tests/test_mp3.py` holds the scanner and the frame to files made by hand and the route to the digest, and `tools/audio/seek_e2e.py` measures in Chrome where a seek lands, by ear. The aligner's repair has two more passes for what the first leaves (`transcribe.py`: `--short`, the units under five words and the headings, with a window that reaches over the transcript's silences; `--near`, the 26 s round a named unit's start, one piece of Whisper's; CLAUDE.md trap 49).
- **The translations, read by the browser's voice** (2026-10-27; `DECISIONS.md`): `build/speech.py` `derive_tr` builds `tr_speech`, each translation's paragraphs as written but for what a voice would trip on (`speak_ru`: the stress mark and soft hyphens out, digit groups joined, a power, a fraction, a degree and a few abbreviations in words, a Latin list mark as its number, capitals capitalized, brackets out; numbers otherwise digits), and a heading per section it titles (`heading_ru`). A language with no such function fails the build, and `check_audio` holds every translated paragraph to its spoken text and that text to its language's alphabet (`ALLOWED_TR`). `/speech/<key>/<paper>:<section>` sends it in `/speech/`'s shape. In `listen.js` a translation heard is the voice `browser:<key>`, its paragraphs those whose row's fold has it (`.tf[data-has]`, `trIds()`), spoken by `S.voice(lang, key)`, the voice Settings gives it (`trvoices`, JSON in a string) or the browser's best in its language (`ub.js voices(lang)`). It is started by the ▶ `trPlay()` puts in each of its cells when `ub.js cmpDraw()` says Compare is drawn (`ub:cmp`), by the one `foldPlay()` puts in a paragraph's fold when `trDraw()` has drawn it (`ub:trfold`, since 2026-11-13), or chosen in the bar's list, never by a setting; carrying on keeps it while the next section has it. `ub.js ubTrRead()` gives it the line being read, in Compare's column or the paragraph's fold, which it opens with Follow along on and shuts behind it. Settings' "Listening controls" (`listen`) turns every ▶ on or off, `data-listen="off"` hiding them; it was the English's voice `off` until 2026-10-27, which the head script and `ub.js` read as it turned off. Offline, each translation's download keeps it, `tr-<key>-speech-<n>`, twenty papers to a chunk (`offline/pages.py`); `offline-core.js` answers from it where the translation is kept and leaves the network's failure where not, and `check.mjs` holds both.
- The player is `static/listen.js` (~45 KB, section pages and the whole paper's, which is one section to it: `app.py whole_audio`, and below, The whole paper). A paragraph's ▶ starts it there, the first paragraph's from the spoken heading, and the voice is changed in the bar as well as in Settings (`DECISIONS.md`). While it plays with Follow along off, it also follows the site's links inside the page, so the reader can go anywhere and the reading goes on (`DECISIONS.md`). Settings, the ⚙ dialog, are declared in `ubsite/settings.py`, run by `ub.js`, stored in `localStorage` under `ub_settings`, and applied before first paint (`DECISIONS.md`).
- **A paragraph's ▶ and its time** (2026-11-17; `DECISIONS.md`, The text on the page). `listen.js build()` `place(p, i, label, to)` makes the button and puts it in the stack after the reference; where the reading is cut at the paragraph it sums the segments from `i`, where the ▶ starts (a section's first paragraph's at its spoken heading), to `to`, the paragraph's own, and keeps the milliseconds as `data-ms` and the time at the speed chosen as `data-t` (`clock()`: `m:ss`, to the nearest second, never 0:00); the `ub:settings` handler writes `data-t` again when `rate` changes, and `build()` runs again for another voice. The browser's voice, whose segments have no times, has none, nor a translation's ▶ (`trButton`). The stylesheet does the rest (`ub.css .playp`): the triangle 1.1rem in a button of 1.4rem, about the centre the smaller one had, so every rule that places it moved by .125rem; in the stack (`.stk > .playp`) a flex row, and with the references shown and the 1955 pages not, on any paragraph but a paper's opening and a line of verse, as wide as the stack and `--lh` tall, the triangle at its start and `::after`, `content: attr(data-t)`, at its end, in the reference's type and grey, lining figures (a button does not inherit the page's), offset as `.pg` is so that it stands on the line's baseline. `main:has(.playp[data-t]) .num` is at least 2.9rem wide, for references of five characters. The opacity is the `svg`'s, not the button's. Help's example carries the time from `app.py play_times()`, the same sum over `section_audio()`. `tests/test_play.py` holds the static facts and `tools/play_e2e.py` drives it in Chrome, against the app on 127.0.0.1:8138; not a gate.

## The text API

Since 2026-10-11. `/api/v1` lets other sites keep their copy of the English text in step with ours, and `/api` documents it, with a client in Python.

- **The protocol is a text version.** A site keeps the number it was sent and asks `/api/v1/text?since=N`. It gets every record new or changed since N, the keys of any removed, and the new number. From 0 it gets the whole text, 3 MB gzipped.
- **A record** is a part, a paper, a section or a paragraph, each with a hash. A paragraph also has `html`, `uf_ref`, `page_1955`, `separator_before`, `group_before`, `kind`, `setoff` and `readings_1955`. The `html` is `render.py`'s, with an empty `<span class="v1955">` for each 1955 reading in place of the site's note link.
- **`build/versions.py`** keeps the versions in an append-only ledger, `data/text/`, because `ub.db` remembers no earlier build. The ledger is appended only after a build passes.
- **`build/foundation.py`** gives every paragraph and section the Foundation's reference, `uf_ref`. It reads its SRT HTML (the zip in the press repo) and aligns it with ours paper by paper. Its differences must be exactly `tests/known-uf-refs.tsv`.
- **`ubsite/api.py`** only concatenates stored records. Each worker gzips a reply once. Replies carry an ETag, so an unchanged one is a 304, and `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`.
- **Calls are counted apart from views**, in `api_call` in `visits.db`. `/stats` lists each caller by its User-Agent.
- **A passage or a paper by reference** (2026-10-13): `/api/v1/paragraphs/<refs>` and `/api/v1/papers/<numbers>` send the same stored records, in reading order, each once, with the section, paper and part around them. `api.py` `_book` places every paragraph in reading order once per worker, a selection becomes runs of places (`_merge`), and `_select` caches each reply by its runs, so equivalent selections share a reply and an ETag. The text version does not move.

## The 1955 readings and their reasons

Since 2026-10-12. Each `\fnc` is a small ring above the words that changed; a click opens a card under the line with the 1955 reading **as the press gives it** and the **SRT Committee's decision and reasons**. The margin holds the index alone. **Only the press says what 1955 printed** (Tigran, 2026-10-12): the committee's page has errors of its own, in quoting 1955 among them (trap 21), so it supplies its reasons, under its name, and nothing else. Its quotation of the 1955 text pairs a note with its entry and is never shown.

- `tools/srt/snapshot.py` (`make srt-snapshot`) keeps the two tables of the Fellowship's page (`sources.toml [srt]`) in `data/srt/considerations.html`, verbatim, with the URL, fetch time and page hash in its header. The build reads the snapshot, never the network, and records it in `build_source` as `srt`.
- `build/srt.py` reads it into `srt_item`, one row per change (an entry may hold several: 134:3.3 has three), maps the page's references (the Foundation's numbering) to ours through `uf_paragraph`, rewrites its links to our paragraphs, and pairs each note with its change (`footnote.srt_item_id`). What does not pair one to one is exactly `tests/known-srt-deltas.tsv`.
- `section.html` puts the notes after the text as endnotes (what a reader sees without JavaScript, and in print); `ub.js` opens each as a card at its ring, positioned in `<article>` so it scrolls with the text. `ubsite/queries.py` decides what a note shows.
- **A finger's drag on a card is the card's** (Tigran, 2026-11-12: on his phone, dragging on past the end of a long note moved the text under it). The words, `.pop .body`, are held by `overscroll-behavior: contain`, which Chrome 157 honours with a touch screen however it was tried: a drag, a flick, one stroke past the end, a new stroke at the end. But the strip above them, the ×'s, 1.65rem of the card, is not part of what scrolls, and a drag begun there moved the page; that was the one way found to do it, and what his phone did could not be seen from here. So `ub.js popTouch`, on every card as it is made (`open`, `pvOpen`), decides a stroke at its first move, once: where the words can scroll that way the browser has it, as ever; where they cannot, at their end, in a card too short to scroll, or on the strip, the stroke's `touchmove` is cancelled, and being no scroll at all it passes to nothing, whatever a browser makes of the stylesheet (tried with the containment taken away). Two fingers are left to the browser, a pinch, and so is a stroke while words are selected, a long press and its drag. The listener is the one in `ub.js` that is not passive, and it is on the card, never the document, so that no other touch on the page waits for script. `tests/test_previews.py` holds this, and `tools/preview_e2e.py` drives the drags in Chrome, which is not a gate.
- **Search finds them too** (Tigran, 2026-10-12), by the reading and by the reasons its card shows: `footnote_fts` (`004_fts.sql`, filled by `derive.fts`), one row per note, never the committee's quotation of 1955 and none of its entries that have no note. `/search` lists the hits as "In the 1955 readings": the reading, today's words around the ring, the committee's words where they matched, and a link, `/93:5.8#note93_5_8_0`, that lands on the paragraph with the card open (`#note`, not the ring's own `#fnref`, which the browser would scroll to itself, over `ub.js`). The list is always sent and shown only when Settings' "1955 readings in search results" is on (off by default), so no setting reaches the server. 48 readings hold a word today's text never uses: *Badanon*, *Tannach*, *forego*, *commerical*.
- **The List of 1955 readings**, `/lists/1955` (Tigran, 2026-10-12): all 180 by paper, each with its reading, today's words around the ring, and the committee's verdict. Its rings are the text's own `a.fn` and open the same card in place, and so do the rings among the search results: `ub.js` frames a card in `main.results` where there is no `<article>`. A note is selected once, by **`queries.notes()`**, for a section, the list and a search, and drawn once, by `templates/_note.html`; a list line is `templates/_old.html`. The list keeps its rings with the readings turned off in the text, because a reader asked for it.

## Justified, hyphenated where the book hyphenates

Since 2026-10-12. The text is set justified, and a word breaks at a line's end only where the press lets it. `build/hyphen.py` runs the press's own model: Knuth's `hyphen.tex` (vendored from TeX Live, `vendor/tex/`), hyphenmins 2/3, the 523 entries of `tex/urantia-hyphen-en.tex`, and the source's 1,392 `\-`, which the expander now keeps as zero-width `shy` spans. `paragraph.html_hy` is `paragraph.html` with a U+00AD at each of those points, and only the section page uses it. `html` and the API are byte-identical to before, so the text version did not move.

- `build/checks/check_hyphen.py` gates it: removing every U+00AD gives `html`; every `\-` is kept; nothing breaks under the minimums; every listed word breaks as its entry says.
- `tools/hyphen_print.py` holds it to a built PDF of the book. On 2026-10-12 all 23,494 breaks printed in words of the text were offered, against 1,217 missed with the patterns alone. It is not a gate, because the PDF can be stale (CLAUDE.md §5).
- Ragged right is a setting (`DECISIONS.md`). Chrome copies soft hyphens, so `ub.js` takes them out of the selected text for the moment of a copy and puts them back before the next paint. The copy is then the browser's own: plain text and HTML, with its styles, byte-identical to a copy from the page without them (checked 2026-10-12: one paragraph, three with the margin between, part of a word, red letter with a 1955 ring, dot-leader rows, verse, the whole page).
- **A paragraph's last word is left whole** (2026-10-31; `DECISIONS.md`, The text on the page): `Hyphenator.points()` puts no break in the text's last run of letters, but for a `\-` in it, so that the whole word drops to the last line and its stub never stands there alone ("lute."). 14,890 soft hyphens fewer (515,975), and the translations' the same way (`RUSSIAN`). `check_hyphen` fails a last word broken otherwise, the translations' too, and `tools/hyphen_print.py` gives each such word the model's own points, so that the print is still compared with the model. The same day `text-wrap: pretty` went out of the justified text (`ub.css` `p.t`, `.rt`; kept under Ragged right): Chrome's keeps a single word off the last line by carrying a word, or a hyphenated piece of one, down to it, and on a phone's 30-character line left the line above stretched, "7. The Unquali-" over "fied Absolute." (0:3.8). In headless Chrome at 360 px it moved a word that fitted in 1 to 8 lines of each of Paper 0's first sections, the greedy fill in none.

**A study note's Greek breaks too** (Tigran, 2026-11-05, after a card at 9:5.7 whose unbreakable κεκαυστηριασμένων had spread the line before it across the card): its `\textgreek` spans, `lang="grc"` in the HTML, are hyphenated by `hyphen.py`'s `GREEK`, Dimitrios Filippou's patterns for ancient Greek (`vendor/tex/hyph-grc.tex`, from TeX Live by value) with the minimums polyglossia gives a language whose gloss sets none, 2 and 3, which is what `\selectlanguage{greek}` with variant=ancient does; `tests/test_hyphen.py` holds the model to one `\showhyphens` line of xelatex's for the notes' words. The patterns list each accented vowel with tonos and with oxia, so a word is looked up as written; one with a combining mark is left whole, as a Russian one with a stress mark is, and so is a note's last word. The study edition itself sets `\textgreek` as a font switch under English, so this is the site's model, not the edition's. The card's gate, `check_db`, now allows a soft hyphen inside a word of any script. The book's own text has no Greek spans, so its 515,975 soft hyphens did not move.

## Reading paths

Since 2026-10-13. `/i/<slug>/read` reads a headword's citations as one text: every paragraph it cites, once, in the order of the book, with the entries citing it beside it; `/i/<slug>/read/theme` reads the same paragraphs a chapter to each of the index's level-2 groups. A concept page links its path as "Read it through", with its length.

- `derive.reading_path` builds `reading_path`: a row for each A–Z headword with a path (`PATH_MIN`, 3 paragraphs: 2,058 of 4,736), with its paragraphs, words and chapters. A headword has a path if it has a row.
- **`queries.reading_path()`** arranges it, ported from the mockup's generator: running heads where the section changes, a fold for what a section has between two paragraphs drawn, a one-line pointer back for a paragraph drawn above (By theme only), a signpost for a paper cited whole, verse grouped as `queries.section` groups it. Captions follow the margin's rule: a range at its first paragraph only (`opens`).
- `templates/path.html` draws it with the section page's own paragraph, `templates/_para.html`'s `para()`, moved out of `section.html` (every section page byte-identical after, checked 2026-10-13). A fold's copy of a paragraph drawn elsewhere has no id, so each paragraph's reference is its id once.

## Accounts

Signing in with Google (2026-11-18), each site's own, and what follows the account (2026-11-19): a reader's settings, all but the device's own, the place last read and the bookmarks, the same on every device signed in. **The module** is `ubsite/account.py`, which knows nothing of requests: `Config` (`var/account.toml`: the site's own origin and Google's client, read again when the file changes; `python -m ubsite.account setup` writes it, mode 0600), and `Accounts` over `var/user.db`, opened as `board.py` opens its file, a connection to an operation, WAL, `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`, with `secure_delete` as `visits.py` has it. Tables: `user` (Google's `sub`, the address and name as last given, made, seen, the terms agreed to and when), `session` (the cookie's SHA-256, renewed, expires), `pending` (what Google said of an account not made yet, for ten minutes), `allow` (a feature given to an address, bound to the account at its first sign-in), `item` (what follows the account, below), `meta` (an id made with the file). `begin()` makes the address at Google and the state to keep; `finish()` checks the state, has `fetch_token()` exchange the code (urllib, a timeout; the one place the server asks another, and the function a test replaces), reads the claims (`claims_of`), and answers a session for an account there is or a pending token for one to be made; `agree()` makes it; `who()` is the question everything else asks.

**The picture** (`user.photo`, `photo_type`): `claims_of` takes Google's `picture` only where it is an address Google keeps pictures at (`PICTURES`, `Config.pictures`); `fetch_photo()` gets a copy 96 px square, at most 64 kB, of four image types, and answers nothing for any failure. It is fetched in `finish()` for an account there is, before the database is held, and in `agree()` for a new one, whose `pending` row holds only the address meanwhile; `who()` hands it on as a `data:` URL. A `user.db` made before a column gains it (`_con`).

**The routes** are in `app.py`, beside the board's: `/account` (the page), `/account/login`, `/account/callback`, `/account/welcome` (the tick), `POST /account/agree`, `/account/done`, `GET /account/me`, `POST /account/logout`, `POST /account/sync` (what follows the account, below), `POST /account/delete`. Every answer is `no-store`, `noindex`, `no-referrer`. A POST must come from the site's own origin as JSON (`_acct_post`). The callback answers a page whatever fails, never a 500, which the worker would replace with the copy's 404. With no `var/account.toml` all of them are 404. **What follows the account** (2026-11-19; `account.py sync()`, `static/account.js`). The browser's copy stays the working copy: `ub_settings`, `ub_place` and `ub_marks` as `ub.js` keeps them, what a page is drawn from before it is painted and what works offline. Nothing in how a page is drawn changed. The server holds a replica, a row of `item` to each thing: the account, a kind (`set` a setting, `place`, `mark` a bookmark), a key (the setting's; none; the bookmark's paragraph), a value as JSON, or NULL for a bookmark removed or a setting back at its default, the account's running number as the row was written (`user.n`), the session that wrote it, and a time. A removal is a row too, so that it reaches the other devices. The settings that follow are every one but `settings.py DEVICE`, the device's own (the translations' folds and their tab following since 2026-11-20): the two text sizes, the line spacing and the width, the browser's voices, and what is folded; the page is handed that list as `window.ubOwn`.

- **One exchange, one address**, `POST /account/sync`: the browser sends `{first, db, n, ch}`, what changed there since last time as `[kind, key, value]` with the last number it saw, and is answered `{db, id, n, had, ch}`, everything changed since that number that it did not just send. With nothing to say, that is how a device learns of the others.
- **The order is the server's, never a browser's clock.** A change is taken where the row is no newer than the number the browser sent, or was written by this same session. Otherwise two devices changed one thing unseen by each other: the place and a bookmark added go by the time they were made, which they carry and which is never counted as later than the server's clock; anything else, a setting or a removal, by which arrived first; and the browser is sent what stands.
- **A first exchange**, `first`, by a device that has never exchanged with the account: the account's settings replace the device's where the account has had an exchange (`had`), and the device's become the account's where it has not, since a setting left out means its default and the two cannot be mixed a setting at a time; every bookmark of both is kept, one here even if it was once removed elsewhere; and the place is the newer. A first exchange that brings nothing still counts as one (`user.n` goes to 1).
- **The exchange's version** (2026-11-20): a request names `v`, the version its script speaks (`account.js V`), and `was`, the version of the script that made the state it keeps (`ub_sync.v`), each 1 where absent, as the first script named none. `settings.LATE` names each setting that came to follow after the first script, with the version that first knew it: a script older than it is neither sent the setting nor heard on it, since the first script noted whatever it was sent and called a setting it did not keep back to its default; for a browser with `was` below it and `v` at it the setting is new, sent whatever its number and what the browser says of it settled as unseen. A setting that comes to follow later goes in `LATE`, and `V` is raised; `tests/test_account.py` holds the list the first script followed, for ever.
- **What the server takes** (`changes_of`): a setting it knows that is not the device's own, its value of the type of its default and not held to today's choices, since a name kept from before is still read as what it meant; a place or a bookmark whose reference is a paragraph of `ub.db`, which is opened to read; words on one line and bounded. Whatever else a browser says is left out and the rest stands: browsers hold the keys of settings long gone. 1,000 changes to a request, 2,000 bookmarks to an account, 1 MB to a body (`app.py SYNC_BODY`, and nginx's location for it in both files, 30 a minute). A browser whose number is ahead of the server's, or whose database is another, a `user.db` restored from a backup or made anew, is answered `restart`, and begins again with `first`.
- **In the browser**, `static/account.js`, its address on the account's menu (`data-src`), run by `ub.js` (`acctFollow`) where the browser's note says someone is signed in, and not on the pages a sign-in passes through: a reader with no account runs none of it. Beside the three keys it keeps `ub_sync`: whose account and which database, the server's number, when, `base`, the things as they stood at the last exchange, and `sent`. What changed here is found by comparing `base` with what is stored now (`changed`), the stored values themselves (`ubSettings.kept`), so a change made in another tab, on a page of an older copy, or offline is found all the same. One exchange at a time across the tabs (`navigator.locks`), 500 changes to one, more in further turns.
- **An answer may never be heard**: the exchange sent as a page is left outlives its page. So what is said is noted first (`sent`), and whatever stands noted is said again in the next exchange, as it then is (`saying`): the server takes a thing said twice as said once. Without it, a setting sent as a page was left and put back on the next page was never sent as put back, and was undone by its own first sending (found by `tools/follow_e2e.py`).
- **When**: as a page loads or comes back into view, if the last exchange was over five minutes ago, or at once where something other than the place waits; three seconds after a setting or a bookmark changes (`ub:settings`, and `ub:kept`, which `ub.js` says as it writes a bookmark or the place); as the page goes out of view with something changed (`visibilitychange` and `pagehide`), which is how the place leaves a device, in a request made at once, with no lock waited for, that may outlive the page (`keepalive`, where it is under 60 KB); when the network returns; and whenever the account's menu is opened (`ubFollow.now`), so that a reader with a device in each hand need not wait five minutes.
- **What comes** is put into the browser's copy a thing at a time (`bring`), each only where it stands as it stood when the exchange began, so that a change made during the round trip is not written over. The settings go through `ubSettings.take`, which keeps them and puts them on the page by the steps a page come back from the browser's cache takes (`ub.js shown()`), Whole paper changed moving the page to its other unit at the section being read; `ub:brought` then has Settings' dialog, the bookmarks' ribbons and list, and the bar redraw.
- **Restore the defaults**, signed in, asks with other words (`reset:account?`): the settings that follow the account go back to their defaults on every device.
- **Signing out** first sends what has not gone (`ubFollow.flush`, four seconds at most), then leaves the browser as it is; with the box under Sign out ticked (`.acct-clear`, for a computer that is not one's own), what followed the account is taken out of the browser too, the device's own settings staying, and the page is read again (`ubFollow.forget`). Signed in again as the same account, the exchange goes on from `ub_sync`. **Another account** signing in where `ub_sync` names the first is asked (`sync:other?`): yes, and everything of the first's in the browser is replaced by the second's, never merged into it, the page read again; no, and the reader is signed out, the browser as it was. An account deleted takes its rows with it, the bytes too, and the browser forgets the exchange and keeps what it has.

**What the pages say of it.** The pages are one for every host, the offline copy's too, and not every host has accounts, so the head script marks `<html>` with `hasacct` where the page's own host is among `ACCOUNT_HOSTS`, and `ub.css` shows `.acctonly` there and `.acctnone` elsewhere. ## The chart of the personalities

Since 2026-11-13 (Tigran: "Please estimate the feasibility", then "go ahead and implement the integration of his data into ubsite"; `DECISIONS.md`, The chart of the personalities). Alexander Alyuev's "Cosmology of Personalities in the Urantia Book" (https://github.com/alyuev/UniverseGenealogy_UrantiaBook_2D) is 120 orders of personalities, each a card where it has its origin, in 25 units of the universe nested from Paradise to the mortal and his Adjuster, twelve of them drawn again where they serve, with 153 lines of origin, service and ascent. His repository holds it as YAML, a structure and a file of words to a language, English and Russian, and an engine that lays it out and routes its lines in the reader's browser. The site draws it itself, at `/cosmology`, from a snapshot of his data, and credits him for the data, "Data source by Alexander Alyuev" (Tigran, 2026-11-14; `ru.toml cosmo:by`, `home:cosmology`, `help:chart:1`).

**The source and its snapshot.** `sources.toml` `[cosmology]` names his repository, cloned beside the others, a master, and `config.cosmology()` gives its commit and the snapshot's path; `config.load()` passes it by, since the build never reads it. `make cosmo-snapshot` (`tools/cosmo/snapshot.py`, a tool of this machine: PyYAML and Playwright's Chrome) reads it at that commit through `readers/_io.py` (`read_at_rev`, and `list_at_rev` for `data/lang/`), each file held to the keys the tool knows, so that a field of his is never read past. His data holds no geometry: a card's place follows from the tree by a rule, and where a line runs is found as his page opens, by an A* search over a 10 px grid with two passes of rip-up and reroute, two and a half seconds in Chrome here. So the tool gives his own built page, `dist/cosmology.html`, to Chrome from memory at a name no site has, every other request refused, once in each language, and asks it for what it drew (`GEOMETRY`: the engine's own `persons`, `GHOSTS`, `UNITS`, `edges`, `W`, `H`, `LONG`): every card's corner, every unit's box and the width its title was drawn at, every line as the corners it turns at. The page's own data must be what the YAML makes by his loader's rule (`merged()`), or the page was built from other data and the tool stops; the boxes must be the same in both languages; the routes are not, a unit's title being an obstacle as wide as his font drew it, and both sets are kept. `data/cosmology/chart.json` is the result, a record to a line, with no date of its own: the same commit makes the same file. `data/cosmology/README.md` describes it.

**The build** (`build/cosmo.py`, `build/readers/cosmo.py`, `build/schema/012_cosmo.sql`), stdlib only, reads the snapshot and nothing of his, after the text, the translations and the index's slugs. `readers/cosmo.py problems()` is its shape: every id one, a personality on one card, every card inside its unit and over no other, every line level or upright from the edge of one card to the edge of another through no third. Then the book: every reference a paper or a section there is. The words: every name and other name in the text, the English in the press's, the Russian in `uf-ru`, which his `ru.yaml` says its terms follow (`TERMS`), by his validator's own rule, a stem with any ending (`found()`); `tests/known-cosmo-terms.tsv` would list a name that is his own wording, and the build wants exactly that list, which is empty. The index: a name that is a headword as written, then another name of its that is, then `data/cosmology/headwords.tsv`, ours, for the rest (`heads()`, `cosmo_head`: 116 of the 120 personalities and 24 of the 25 units). Derived there: a card's sign (`glyph()`, his engine's rule, the first of its groups in `GLYPH_ORDER` deciding it), each line as a path with corners rounded as his `pathD` rounds them (`path_d`), and whether it is far. Tables: `cosmo_meta`, `cosmo_key` (classes, groups, functions), `cosmo_unit`, `cosmo_title`, `cosmo_person`, `cosmo_tag`, `cosmo_place`, `cosmo_link`, `cosmo_route` (by language), `cosmo_text` (every word of his, by language), `cosmo_ref`, `cosmo_head`. `build_source` has the snapshot's commit as `cosmology`; where his repository is on the machine and has moved on, the build warns (`cosmo-snapshot-behind`) and does not fail. `make check` has a section (`checks/check_cosmo.py`), `fonts.py` counts `cosmo_text` among what the faces must draw.

**The page** (`app.py cosmology_page`, made once for a database; `queries.cosmo()`; `templates/cosmology.html`; `ub.css`, the last section). The chart's name and the credit (`.chead`); then the line (`.cbar`): the tools that need scripts, hidden until `cosmo.js` runs, which are the search box (on a phone a magnifier, `.cqb`, that opens it across the line, `.cfind.open`), the scale's two buttons and Whole chart (`.czoom`), four arrows (`.cpan`), each a round `.cbtn` drawn by the template's `btn()`, and the Legend (a `<details>`, its chips the keys; its word on a wide screen, its sign on a phone). One line on every screen: on a phone the wrappers are `display: contents` and the nine buttons flex items that give way together, 29 to 36 px. Then the canvas, 1900 by 6680 in the canvas's own unit, the px, scaled whole by `--cz` (`.csize` the canvas as large as drawn, `.cworld` the canvas itself): one `<svg>` for the units' boxes, the pairs' frames and the lines, each line twice, `<g class="en">` and `<g class="ru">`, of which the stylesheet shows the reader's; over it the units' titles and the cards, each an `<a>`, so that their words are `<x-en>` and `<x-ru>` like the rest of the interface (which cannot stand in SVG text, trap 33) and wrap by CSS, cut at three lines. **The page itself scrolls** (Tigran, 2026-11-14: "the behaviour should be the same as when reading the text, where only the minimal header remains when we scroll down"; `DECISIONS.md`).
